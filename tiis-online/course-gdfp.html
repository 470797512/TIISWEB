Course Overview

Graduate Diploma Overview

The Graduate Diploma of Financial Planning (GDFP) is designed to equip you with the skills and knowledge needed to excel in the financial planning industry. The qualification develops financial-planning knowledge. Completing a degree alone does not authorise you to practise as a financial adviser; examination, professional year, registration and other applicable requirements must also be met.

Study taxation, investment, superannuation, insurance, law, ethics and behavioural finance, then bring these areas together in a financial-plan capstone. Check professional eligibility and examination requirements separately.

Key Learning Outcomes

  • ✓ Develop and present comprehensive financial plans
  • ✓ Understand investment strategies and risk management
  • ✓ Navigate the regulatory and ethical framework for financial advice
  • ✓ Develop the academic foundation for financial planning practice

Plan the sequence

  1. Start with the foundations

    Arrange FP501–FP504 with their listed co-requisites.

  2. Complete the full subject set

    FP505, FP506 and FP507 are also required. Confirm their scheduling with TIIS.

  3. Bring it together in FP508

    Complete FP501–FP504 before the Financial Plan Construction capstone.

The sequence explains dependencies; it does not allocate subjects to fixed terms.

Confirm your duration before enrolling. The current course webpage advertises 8 months full-time / 16 months part-time, while its linked brochure gives different full-time durations. Ask admissions for the current teaching calendar and completion date for your offer.

Admissions

Entry Requirements

Academic

A recognised undergraduate degree or equivalent; a graduate certificate in finance; or a finance-related Diploma/Advanced Diploma plus three years of relevant financial-services experience in the past five years. Admissions will assess the applicable entry route.

English

IELTS overall 6.0 (no band below 6.0), or equivalent.
